What a tetralogy is

Four things are found together, and they follow from one another rather than arriving separately. The wall between the ventricles is incomplete, so the two sides of the heart are not kept apart. The aorta sits over that gap instead of over the left ventricle alone, so it receives blood from both. The way out of the right ventricle towards the lungs is narrowed. And the right ventricle, pushing against that narrowing, thickens.

The outlines on this study name normal anatomy: a ventricle is a ventricle whatever it is doing. What makes the case a tetralogy is how those structures sit relative to one another, which is something to look for by scrolling rather than something any single outline records.

Using the viewer

  • Scroll

    The wheel moves through the stack, and the arrow keys do the same once the image has focus. The heart occupies the middle of the series.

  • Window

    Drag with the left button to change the window. The presets are true Hounsfield values, so Soft tissue and Lung mean the same here as on a workstation.

  • Zoom and pan

    The right button zooms, the middle button pans. Reset puts both back.

  • Identify

    Press Identify, then drag a box round a structure. It is named beside the image and its outline is drawn over the anatomy.

Where this comes from

Images and outlines from ImageCHD (opens in a new tab), a dataset of 110 congenital heart CT studies published by Xiaowei Xu and colleagues and described in their MICCAI 2020 paper (opens in a new tab). Released under Attribution-NonCommercial 4.0 International (CC BY-NC 4.0); this page is non-commercial and carries the attribution the licence asks for.

The dataset ships NIfTI volumes. They were converted to DICOM for this page by scripts/nifti-to-dicom.py, which restores the voxel spacing from the dataset's own spreadsheet, settles the orientation from the anatomy, and shifts the stored values into Hounsfield units. The seven outlines are the published ones, re-encoded rather than redrawn. No patient dates are carried over.
