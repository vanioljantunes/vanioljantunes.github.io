What is a tetralogy of Fallot?

Four things found together, and they follow from one another rather than arriving separately. The wall between the ventricles is incomplete, so the two sides of the heart are not kept apart. The aorta sits over that gap instead of over the left ventricle alone, so it receives blood from both. The way out of the right ventricle towards the lungs is narrowed. And the right ventricle, pushing against that narrowing, thickens.

The outlines on this study name normal anatomy: a ventricle is a ventricle whatever it is doing. What makes the case a tetralogy is how those structures sit relative to one another, which is something to look for by scrolling rather than something any single outline records.

How do I drive the viewer?

The wheel moves through the stack, and the arrow keys do the same once the image has focus; the heart occupies the middle of the series. Dragging with the left button changes the window, and the presets are true Hounsfield values, so Soft tissue and Lung mean here what they mean on a workstation. The right button zooms, the middle button pans, and Reset puts both back.

Identify is the one control that is not a view setting. Press it, drag a box round a structure, and the structure is named beside the image with its outline drawn over the anatomy.

Why does scrolling one study move the other?

Because the point of the page is the comparison. The two children are different sizes and their scans start and stop at different levels, so the link maps between the outlined hearts rather than between slice numbers: it holds the level through the heart and drifts outside it. Window, zoom and pan stay separate, so each study can be set up on its own.

What is the control, and in what sense is it healthy?

ImageCHD case 1080, one of the twenty cases the dataset lists no congenital defect against. That is what the table records, not a reading of the images, and the child was still scanned for some clinical reason. So it is a comparison rather than a certificate of a normal heart.

The pair was chosen to match: the same scanner, the same 0.24 mm voxels and the same 0.75 mm slices, so a difference between them is a difference in the hearts rather than in how they were scanned.

Where do the images and the outlines come from?

ImageCHD, a dataset of 110 congenital heart CT studies published by Xiaowei Xu and colleagues and described in their MICCAI 2020 paper. Released under Attribution-NonCommercial 4.0 International (CC BY-NC 4.0); this page is non-commercial and carries the attribution the licence asks for.

The seven outlines are the published ones, re-encoded rather than redrawn.

How did NIfTI volumes become something the viewer can read?

The dataset ships NIfTI. scripts/nifti-to-dicom.py converts each case to a DICOM series: it restores the voxel spacing from the dataset's own spreadsheet, settles the orientation from the anatomy, and shifts the stored values into Hounsfield units so the window presets mean what they say. No patient dates are carried over.

The page then runs the same Cornerstone3D engine as the other viewers, with its own copy of the bundle and the codec binaries, because the Emscripten decoders resolve their .wasm against the page that loads them.
